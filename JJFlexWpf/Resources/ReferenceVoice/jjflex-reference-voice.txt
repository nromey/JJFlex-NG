JJ Flexible reference voice — script, version 1
================================================

This is the script the shipped reference recording reads, word for word.

It is here for two reasons. An operator can read it to find out exactly what
they are about to transmit, without having to transmit it to find out. And
anyone re-recording the reference — in their own voice, on their own
microphone — says the same words in the same order, so the two recordings stay
comparable.

Lines in square brackets are instructions to the person or the tool making the
recording. They are not spoken. Everything else is read aloud, at a normal
speaking level and a normal speaking distance, without hurrying.

DELIBERATELY NOT ON THIS RECORDING: any callsign, any CQ call, and anything
that would sound like a real contact. If this ever goes out over the air by
accident, it must be obvious to anyone listening that it is a test recording
and not a station calling. That is why it says so twice.


[section: slate]

JJ Flexible reference voice, version one. This is a test recording, not a live
transmission.


[section: tone-announce]

Level tone. One thousand hertz, twenty decibels below full scale.

[tone: 1000 Hz, -20 dBFS, 2 seconds]


[section: steady]

Steady passage, for the loudness meters.

On a good evening the band opens just before sunset and stays open for hours,
and you can work a station three thousand miles away with a wire in a tree and
a hundred watts. On a bad evening the same wire hears nothing but noise. The
difference is not the radio. It is the sky, the season and the sun, and no
amount of adjusting a transmitter will change any of the three. What you can
change is how you sound, and that is what this recording is for.


[section: phonetics]

Phonetics. Alpha, Bravo, Charlie, Delta, Echo, Foxtrot, Golf, Hotel, India,
Juliett, Kilo, Lima, Mike, November, Oscar, Papa, Quebec, Romeo, Sierra, Tango,
Uniform, Victor, Whiskey, X-ray, Yankee, Zulu.


[section: counting]

Counting. One, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine, ten.


[section: dynamics-announce]

Dynamic range. The same sentence quietly, normally, then loudly.

[section: dynamics-quiet | gain: -12 dB]

How do you copy my audio?

[section: dynamics-normal | gain: 0 dB]

How do you copy my audio?

[section: dynamics-loud | gain: +6 dB]

How do you copy my audio?


[section: plosives]

Plosives and sibilance. Peter piped a passing barge past the bridge. She sells
sea shells, and she ships them south. Ticking, tapping, popping, stopping.


[section: silence-announce]

Four seconds of silence, for the noise floor.

[silence: 4 seconds]


[section: close]

End of the JJ Flexible reference voice. This was a test recording.
