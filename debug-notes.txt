WHAT CHANGED

The sample rate your microphone actually runs at is now settled with the
device before anything is built from it. It used to be assumed to be 48 kHz,
and if your microphone was running at some other rate, nothing noticed. Your
audio still went out, just built on the wrong number - which sounds like a
regular stutter rather than like anything being broken, so it was easy to
blame on the network.

If your microphone will not open at all, JJ Flex now says so once, out loud,
and keeps receive audio running. Before, it said nothing whatsoever: you would
key up, transmit silence, and be told nothing was wrong.

The Microphone Check now tells you when Windows has your microphone at a rate
the radio link cannot use - 44.1 kHz being the one that turns up. The check
itself is perfectly happy at that rate, so a microphone could pass the check
completely and still be unable to transmit. Now it says so before you find
out the hard way.

WHAT TO TEST

Start the Microphone Check - either the Check Microphone button in the Audio
Workshop, or Start microphone check in Audio Devices - and read the FIRST
line it shows.

If it says only "Microphone check running. Listening." then your microphone is
at 48 kHz and all is well. That is the expected result.

If it goes on to mention 44.1 kHz, that is worth knowing and worth telling me
about, because it means Windows has that device at a rate that will not carry
transmit audio.

After that: talk into it, watch the reading, then go quiet and confirm it
reports silence rather than inventing a level.

With a radio: connect, turn PC Audio on, and listen for the absence of an
announcement about your microphone. Silence is the pass here. Then key up with
the transmit monitor on and listen specifically for a REGULAR, rhythmic gap in
your own voice - not random dropouts, a steady stutter. That rhythm was the
signature of what got fixed. If you hear it, I want to know immediately.
