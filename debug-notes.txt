WHAT CHANGED

This build is almost entirely the result of one morning of Noel sitting in
front of the previous one with NVDA. Every item below came from that.

The Audio Workshop's sections are real groups now. You hear yourself entering
and leaving them as you tab, and the H key jumps between them. They looked
like sections before and were not ones - the walk-through was correctly
ordered and you could not feel yourself crossing from one step to the next.

The device picker only shows things you could talk into. Loopbacks and virtual
audio cables are hidden unless you turn on "show every sound endpoint" with
Alt+S. Nothing is removed, only hidden, so if you route audio deliberately
your devices are still there behind that switch.

Each device is spoken once. It was being said twice - your screen reader
announced the row and then the app said it again.

Device rows start with the device. Press the first letter of a device name and
the list jumps to it, the way a list should.

There is a Windows input level slider in the Microphone Check now, and a
Microphone Boost slider when your sound driver offers one. This is the missing
piece: if your microphone is too hot or too quiet, that is the knob that fixes
it, and until now the app could tell you about the problem but not do anything
about it.

The check reports loudness as well as peak, so it can tell you when you have
stopped being too quiet rather than only when you are clipping.

The level advice has seven levels instead of three, and it talks like a
person. Every answer starts with one word - Good, Hot, Clipping, Quiet - so you
can stop listening as soon as you have what you need, and the rest of the
sentence varies so you are not hearing the same line eight times while you
tune.

Your settings cannot get lost between two folders any more. Some were being
written to one place and read from another, and which ones survived a restart
depended on which part of the app happened to run last.

The level meter now zeroes itself when you move the level. Without that the
check kept reporting the loudest thing it had heard since you started it, which
was from before you touched anything - so every adjustment looked like it did
nothing at all. Found by Noel within minutes of the sliders arriving.

WHAT TO TEST

Open the Audio Workshop with Ctrl+Shift+W and tab through it slowly. You should
hear the section names as you cross into them.

Open Change Audio Devices and count what is in the input list. Confirm your
laptop's own microphone array is still there - that one matters.

Start the microphone check, find the Windows input level slider, and use it to
bring yourself into the Good range. Talk normally.

Then change a setting, close the app completely, and open it again. It should
still be there.

With a radio: connect, turn PC audio on, key up with the transmit monitor on
and listen for a steady, rhythmic gap in your own voice. Random dropouts are
something else; a regular rhythm is the thing that got fixed.

KNOWN ISSUES

The transverter work is not fleshed out yet.

The noise gate and RNNoise are not in this build.

Microphone Boost has only been proven on hardware that does NOT offer one. If
your headset exposes a boost control, you are the first to see it work.

The help page on the two audio numbers still opens with a draft marker. That is
deliberate - it has not had a human pass yet.
