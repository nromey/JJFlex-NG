Hello Don,

This is a big jump from the build you have been running, so the short
version first: the transmit test tone no longer wobbles, your key
assignments will be repaired for you automatically, and there are two
specific things I would like you to try.

Take your time. Nothing here is urgent and nothing here can hurt your
radio.


WHAT IS FIXED THAT YOU WILL NOTICE

The test tone used to gallop. It had a wobble to it, a sort of slow
metronome about three times every four seconds, and it was there in what
your radio actually transmitted, not just in what you heard back. That is
gone. It was fixed on Sunday night and confirmed at a real radio on
Monday morning.

If you ever heard your own audio sounding uneven and could not explain
it, that may well have been the same fault, because the same problem
affected a live microphone too.


YOUR KEYBOARD WILL BE FIXED FOR YOU

The build you have been using is old enough that a change we made on the
18th of August shifted twenty-two of the key assignments onto the wrong
commands. On your machine that would mean some keys quietly did the wrong
thing. Control M, for example, would have run the antenna tuner instead
of opening the meters.

This build detects that and repairs it on the first run, silently. It
only repairs keys you had never customised, so nothing you deliberately
chose gets touched. If you HAD customised a key that got caught in the
shift, it leaves that one alone and writes a note in the diagnostic log
rather than guessing what you meant.

You do not need to do anything. It just happens. But if a key does
something you did not expect, tell me rather than assuming it is you.


THE FIRST THING I WOULD LIKE YOU TO TRY

This tells us whether the problem is at your microphone or somewhere
after it. It is two runs of the same thing with one difference, and you
key the radio yourself both times.

1.  Connect to your radio and turn PC audio on as you normally would.

2.  Press Control J, then the letter G. That arms the test tone. The tone
    is generated inside JJ Flexible, so it goes out INSTEAD of your
    microphone. Your microphone is completely out of the path.

3.  Key up for about five seconds, then unkey.

4.  Press Control J then G again to disarm the tone. Your microphone
    comes back.

5.  Key up again for about five seconds and talk normally, then unkey.

What I want to know is simply whether those two sound different at the
far end, or on a receiver you can hear.

If the tone gets out and your voice does not, the problem is on the
microphone side and we can go looking there. If neither gets out, your
microphone is not the problem at all and we look further along. If both
get out, we are chasing something that comes and goes.

Any of those three answers is useful. There is no wrong result.


THE SECOND THING, AND THIS ONE ONLY YOU CAN ANSWER

You use JAWS, and that turns out to matter in a way we did not know
about until this weekend.

JAWS does not pass a held key through the way NVDA does. Instead of one
long press it sends a rapid series of separate presses and releases,
about four a second. We found this in another project, where a user
reported that holding an arrow key would not make something move.

The worry is push to talk. If JAWS treats Control Space that way, then
holding it to talk would key and unkey your transmitter about four times
a second, and you would have no way of knowing, because as far as you are
concerned you are holding the key down and speaking.

We do not know yet whether it does this. It might only do it for the
arrow keys.

So: hold Control Space and talk for about ten seconds, then let go. Then
tell me whether your audio sounded continuous at the far end, or chopped.

This build watches for it either way and writes a line in the diagnostic
log if it sees it, so if you would rather just use the radio normally and
send me a log afterwards, that works too.


IF YOU WANT TO SEND SOMETHING TO FLEX

There is a transmit chain check that produces a block of text you can
paste into an email. Audio Workshop is Control Shift W, then Control Tab
across to Diagnostics, then the button called Check My Transmit Chain.
Below the result there is a second box headed Evidence to send to Flex
support, and a button that copies it.

It carries your radio's model, serial number and firmware, and the actual
meter readings with times, so a support engineer can check them
themselves.

One thing worth knowing about that text. If you can, try the same
transmission in SmartSDR first. If it fails there too, say so in your
email, because that single sentence is what turns it from a question
about our software into a question about your radio. And if it works fine
in SmartSDR and not here, please do not write to Flex at all, write to
me, because that means the fault is ours.

HOW TO SEND ME A DIAGNOSTIC LOG

Control J then Control D starts and stops a detailed capture from
anywhere, including inside a dialog. Saved captures are in Settings, then
Diagnostics, then the Saved Diagnostic Logs window.
