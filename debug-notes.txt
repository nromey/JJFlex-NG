This build exists mainly to get a current JJ Flexible onto the laptop, so the
orphan-process question can finally be tested on the machine where it actually
happens.

WHAT'S NEW SINCE YOUR LAST BUILD

- There is a warning sound now, and it sounds like a warning. One long tone with
  harmonics, three quarters of a second, deliberately unlike every other sound
  the app makes. It has its own switch in Settings, Notifications, under Alert
  Sounds, alongside the five that were already there.

- Connecting to a radio with no microphone profile selected now says so. On a
  radio you have marked as yours it quietly loads one and tells you; on anyone
  else's it changes nothing and just tells you what it found. Either way you
  hear about it before you key up rather than afterwards.

- The Audio Workshop's Earcon Explorer has a Warnings section: the alarm, the
  problem-recorded tone, and the two feature toggles right beneath them so you
  can hear them back to back.

- The Remove Radio dialog reads better. "Remove from the list only keeps all of
  it" had nothing you were still holding for "it" to point at.

THE ONE THING WORTH DOING ON THE LAPTOP

The orphan-process test. Full procedure is in the repo at
docs\planning\for-noel\2026-08-19-orphan-process-test.md, but the short version:

  1. Settings, Audio: set PC audio for the radio to "Always on for this radio".
  2. Connect, and CONFIRM YOU CAN ACTUALLY HEAR RADIO AUDIO. This is the part
     that makes the test mean anything - switched on is not the same as running.
  3. Leave it playing half a minute, then close the app normally.
  4. Task Manager, or PowerShell: does a jjflexible.exe still exist?

If one survives, do not kill it straight away - note its CPU and thread count
first. Burning CPU is one bug, sitting idle is a different one, and the evidence
goes away with the process.

The laptop is the only machine where that survivor has ever been seen. The
ms-02 has never shown it, which is why testing there proved nothing.

KNOWN ISSUES IN THIS BUILD

- The meters panel (Ctrl+M) is rough. Adding a slot leaves the earlier slots
  with no controls at all, the Test button starts a tone that will not stop, and
  pan offers only left, centre and right. All known, all being worked.

- Choosing "Remove the radio and its settings" may quietly do the gentler thing
  and keep them. Nothing gets destroyed unexpectedly, so it fails in the safe
  direction, but your choice may not be honoured. Under investigation.

- Meter tone slots cannot be reordered, inserted between, or removed with the
  Delete key.
