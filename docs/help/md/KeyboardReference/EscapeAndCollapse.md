# Escape and Collapse

Press `Escape` to back out of what you are doing in JJ Flexible Radio Access. On the JJ Flexible Home, it closes field groups. If you are transmitting, adjusting a control, or using a JJ key layer, `Escape` deals with that activity first.

## Single Escape

When you are inside an expanded DSP, Audio, Receiver, Transmission, or Antenna group, press `Escape` once to collapse that group. Focus moves to its header, and you will hear a short descending chirp.

When focus is on the JJ Flexible Home rather than inside a group, one press closes every open group. If no group is open, `Escape` returns focus to Home.

## Double Escape (Collapse All)

While you are inside an expanded group, press `Escape` twice in quick succession to collapse every open group. Focus returns to the JJ Flexible Home, and you will hear two descending tones: a higher tone followed by a lower one. They tell you that all the groups have closed.

The Double-Tap Tolerance setting determines how close together the two presses must be. Its default is 500 milliseconds between presses. See the Double-Tap Tolerance help topic for more about this setting.

## Why Escape Works This Way

For the JJ Flexible Home's field groups, `Escape` works in two ways:

- One press closes the group you are in. From Home, one press closes every open group.
- Two quick presses from inside a group close every open group and return focus to Home.

`Escape` may deal with something else before closing a group. If a voice message or push-to-talk transmission is running, it stops that transmission first. If you are adjusting a filter edge or the RIT or XIT scale, it cancels that adjustment first. In a JJ key layer, it cancels the layer; in an ordinary dialog, it closes the dialog. During an Audio Check or transmit test, the first press stops the transmission and leaves its window open.

## Re-expanding a Collapsed Group

After you collapse a group with `Escape`, focus rests on the group's header. Press `Space` to expand it again. You will hear an ascending chirp rather than the descending chirp used for collapse.

## What You Will Hear

- **When one group collapses,** you will hear a descending chirp from 1200 hertz to 400 hertz over 350 milliseconds. A gritty noise sweep helps the sound stand out against radio audio.
- **When one group expands,** you will hear a chirp rise from 400 hertz to 1200 hertz with the same texture.
- **When all groups collapse after two presses,** you will hear a higher tone at 1200 hertz followed by a lower one at 400 hertz. Together they last about half a second. This sound differs from the single-group chirps so you can tell which kind of collapse occurred.

## Adjusting the Timing

If you need more or less time between the two `Escape` presses, change **Double-Tap Tolerance** under **Settings > Accessibility**. This setting also controls how quickly you must press `[` or `]` twice to enter filter-edge adjustment mode.
