# Questions for Don — the transverter listening trick

Date: 2026-08-07. From the JJ Flex audio workshop planning session.

Don, Noel mentioned you have a way to hear your own transmitted audio using the transverter port — listening to your actual signal inside one radio, without a transverter connected. We want to build this into JJ Flex as a one-button "check my audio" feature, so nobody has to set it up by hand. Before we build it, we want to make sure we automate the real procedure, not our guess at it.

Eight questions. Short answers are fine — a voice memo or rough notes are equally welcome.

1. Which radio were you on when you did the transverter listening trick — your 6300, or a different Flex? If you have done it on more than one model, which ones?

2. Walk us through it step by step, like you were teaching it on the air. Where do you turn the transverter on, what do you set it to (frequency, level, anything else), and what do you do next?

3. The second receiver that hears your signal — is that a second slice on the same radio? If so, what antenna do you give that slice, and what frequency do you tune it to?

4. Do you hear yourself live while you are talking, or do you listen back afterward?

5. While you are doing this, is your signal also going out over your regular antenna, or does it only come out the transverter port? In other words: could you talk freely without putting anything on the air?

6. Does the trick work when you are connected over SmartLink, and if so, how does the audio sound that way?

7. Noel remembers that Jim's original JJ software had something related to this, and that it was buggy or stopped working. Do you remember what Jim's version did, and what was wrong with it?

8. You have been checking your transmit audio against an online SDR lately. When you compare that with the transverter trick and with the radio's TX monitor, what does each one tell you that the others do not? If JJ Flex could do one thing to make checking your audio easier, what would it be?

Thanks, Don. Your answers decide how we build this — the plan is to automate whatever you do by hand today.
