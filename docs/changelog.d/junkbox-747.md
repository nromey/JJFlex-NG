- Filter widths above 1 kilohertz now say exactly what they are, so stepping a filter 50 hertz at a time never repeats a width or reads one 50 hertz off. At Terse verbosity you hear the short form, like "Filter 100 to 2850, 2.75 k"; at Chatty you hear the whole number with a word to say what it is, like "Filter 100 to 2850, width 2750 hertz". The width readouts in the filter preset list and the Audio Workshop show the exact value too.
- Choosing a filter preset, with Alt and a bracket or from the Filter menu, now says the preset's name once and then the filter it set, like "Wide, filter 100 to 2800, width 2700 hertz" at Chatty or "Wide, 2.7 k" at Terse. It used to say the name twice and leave out the edges. What you hear is the filter you actually got: if the mode holds a preset back you hear where it stopped, "at limit", and in FM, where the filter cannot be changed, you hear that it stayed as it was. Speak Status names the active preset with the filter that is on the radio right now, and the Filter menu lists each preset with its name once.
- The bracket keys, the filter layer and the Filter menu now tell you the filter you actually got. In FM, where the radio does not let the filter change, you hear "Filter unchanged" and where it stands; in RTTY and the other modes that hold a filter back, you hear where it really stopped, "at limit". Before, they read back the numbers they had asked for, even when the radio never took them.
- The transmit filter now keeps its edges at least 50 hertz apart, as the radio's own rules intend, and every place you set it (the Home fields, the Audio Workshop, the Radio menu, the filter layer and the bracket chords) tells you what it actually became. Before, setting the low edge too close to the high one could slip past that rule, or quietly do nothing while still reading back the number you asked for.
- If the radio is not taking changes right now, a filter change tells you plainly that nothing was changed instead of saying nothing at all. Holding a key down in the filter layer no longer loses a step when the radio is slow to answer, and a correction on the transmit filter fields is the last thing you hear.
