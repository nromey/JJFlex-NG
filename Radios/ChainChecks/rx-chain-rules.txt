# Receive chain rules for JJ Flexible.
#
# Why a radio is making no sound, walked in the order the faults actually bite.
# Same file format, same analyzer and same override path as tx-chain-rules.txt:
# read that file's header for how to write a rule. An operator's own copy at
# %AppData%\JJFlexRadio\rx-chain-rules.txt replaces this one entirely.
#
#
# WHY THIS FILE EXISTS, added 2026-08-25.
#
# The receive check used to be thirty-five lines of C# inside
# FlexBase.SilentRadioAdvisory: an if-ladder of seven conditions, first match
# wins, returning ONE sentence and nothing else. No stage names, no evidence
# block, no way to say what it could NOT check, and not a word of it
# correctable without shipping a new binary.
#
# Sitting beside it was the transmit walk: thirteen stages, twenty-one rules,
# in text an operator can edit. Two implementations of the same idea, and only
# one of them could be improved by anybody who was not building the app.
#
# The ladder's ORDER was right and is preserved exactly, because it encodes
# something real: a mute is worse news than a low level, and both are worse
# news than a routing choice. Say the most disabling thing first.

ruleset: Receive audio check
version: 1


# ── Stage 0 ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

stage: 0 the connection to your radio
about: A Flex makes no audio at all until a client connects to it.

rule: rx-no-radio
in-stage: 0
broken-when: radio-connected is no
verdict: No radio is connected. A Flex makes no sound at all until a client connects to it, and that includes its own headphone jack on the front panel. This catches nearly everybody once.
fix: Connect to your radio, then run this check again.


# ── Stage 1 ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
#
# Mutes before levels, and all-three before any one. An operator who is told
# "the headphone output is muted" and fixes it, only to find the line out was
# muted too, has been sent round twice by a check that knew both answers.

stage: 1 the radio's outputs
about: Whether the radio's own outputs are muted.

rule: rx-everything-muted
in-stage: 1
broken-when: headphone-muted is yes
broken-when: lineout-muted is yes
broken-when: front-speaker-muted is yes
verdict: Every output on the radio is muted: headphones, line out, and the front speaker. Nothing can make a sound in this state.
fix: Unmute the outputs you use. All three are in Settings, Audio.
evidence: headphone-muted
evidence: lineout-muted
evidence: front-speaker-muted

rule: rx-headphone-and-lineout-muted
in-stage: 1
broken-when: headphone-muted is yes
broken-when: lineout-muted is yes
verdict: The headphone and line out outputs are both muted.
fix: Unmute whichever one you listen through. Both are in Settings, Audio.
evidence: headphone-muted
evidence: lineout-muted

rule: rx-headphone-muted
in-stage: 1
broken-when: headphone-muted is yes
verdict: The headphone output is muted.
fix: Unmute it in Settings, Audio.
evidence: headphone-muted

rule: rx-lineout-muted
in-stage: 1
broken-when: lineout-muted is yes
verdict: The line out output is muted.
fix: Unmute it in Settings, Audio.
evidence: lineout-muted


# ── Stage 2 ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

stage: 2 the radio's output levels
about: How loud the radio is set to be, which on most Flex models is the only volume control there is.

rule: rx-both-levels-zero
in-stage: 2
broken-when: headphone-level at most 0
broken-when: lineout-level at most 0
verdict: Both of the radio's output levels are at zero. On a radio with no front panel knob this is the only volume control there is, so nothing is going to make a sound until one of them comes up.
fix: Raise the level for whichever output you listen through, in Settings, Audio.
evidence: headphone-level
evidence: lineout-level

rule: rx-headphone-level-zero
in-stage: 2
broken-when: headphone-level at most 0
verdict: The headphone level is at zero.
fix: Raise it in Settings, Audio.
evidence: headphone-level

rule: rx-lineout-level-zero
in-stage: 2
broken-when: lineout-level at most 0
verdict: The line out level is at zero.
fix: Raise it in Settings, Audio.
evidence: lineout-level

rule: rx-both-levels-very-low
in-stage: 2
broken-when: headphone-level at most 5
broken-when: lineout-level at most 5
verdict: Both of the radio's output levels are very low. There may be sound there, but not enough of it to hear over a room.
fix: Raise the level for whichever output you listen through, in Settings, Audio.
evidence: headphone-level
evidence: lineout-level


# ── Stage 3 ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
#
# Last, because it is a routing CHOICE rather than a fault — except on a
# remote connection, where it is the whole ball game: there is no other path
# for the sound to take.

stage: 3 how the audio reaches you
about: Whether radio audio is being carried to this computer, which on a remote connection is the only way to hear it.

rule: rx-pc-audio-off-on-remote
in-stage: 3
needs: remote-radio is yes
broken-when: pc-audio is no
verdict: Radio audio is not playing through this computer, and you are connected remotely. There is no other way for the sound to reach you: the radio's own headphone jack is at the radio.
fix: Turn PC audio on. Control plus J then A toggles it from anywhere, and it is also in Settings, Audio.
evidence: pc-audio
evidence: remote-radio
